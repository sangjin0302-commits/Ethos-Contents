Verified as of: October 6, 2026

You have received a prior notice saying your real estate agency faces a six-month business suspension. The short answer is that the period is not yet final. It can change, but not because you explain that six months is hard on your business. It changes when the facts, the applicable standard and the grounds for reduction line up, and when you can prove them with records.

## Where the six months comes from

Under Article 39 of the Licensed Real Estate Agents Act(「공인중개사법」), the registration authority may suspend a licensed agent's business for a period of up to six months. The detailed standards are set by ministerial ordinance. Annex 4 of the Enforcement Rule sets a standard period for each type of violation.

So the first thing to read in your notice is not the length but the violation it relies on, and which item of Annex 4 was applied to it. If several violations are listed together, Annex 4 allows the period to be increased within set limits, but the total suspension can never exceed six months.

## When the period can be shortened

Annex 4 also lets the authority reduce the standard period by up to half when:

- the violation is found to be a minor mistake or an error caused by carelessness;
- you made a recognized effort to correct or resolve the violation; or
- the motive, result and severity of the violation justify a shorter period.

For a violation with a six-month standard, that means the period could come down to three months. Note the wording: the authority *may* reduce it. A reduction depends on whether those facts exist and whether your records show them.

## Two decisions that show the difference

In a Gyeonggi administrative appeal decided on December 6, 2021, an agency received a prior notice of a six-month suspension for several violations. The authority then reduced the final suspension to three months, because the agent had no past sanctions and had taken steps to resolve the violations, including completing job training. The agent's later appeal against the three-month suspension was dismissed.

In a Seoul administrative appeal decided on October 21, 2024, the agent had already received two or more suspensions or fines within the past year and then committed another advertising violation. The appeal found the violations were not minor mistakes and that no corrective effort had been shown, so the request to cancel or reduce the suspension was rejected.

## What to check, in order

1. Compare the facts in the notice with your own records: contracts, disclosure statements, listings and filings.
2. Confirm the provision and the Annex 4 standard applied to those facts.
3. List anything that could raise or lower the period: past sanctions, number and severity of violations, and when and how you corrected them.
4. Gather documents that prove each point. A claim without records is hard for the authority to accept.

## Respond before the decision, and check the dates

Under Article 21 of the Administrative Procedures Act, the prior notice comes before the final decision, and you may submit your opinion with supporting evidence. If the authority finds your opinion well-founded, it must take it into account.

Also check when the violation happened. Under Article 39(3), a suspension cannot be imposed once three years have passed since the cause arose. If a final suspension is issued, an administrative appeal is generally due within 90 days of learning of the decision and no later than 180 days after it, subject to the notice of appeal rights and any special rules.

## What you can check yourself, and what needs review

You can read the alleged facts, legal basis and response deadline in the notice, and collect your contracts, disclosure statements, listings and past sanction records. A fuller review helps when several violations are listed together, when the authority's facts differ from your records, when the applicable standard is unclear, or when you need to argue for a reduction from a six-month standard.
